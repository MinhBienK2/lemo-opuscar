# Retro — building Tập 03, twenty minutes long (2026-10-30)

The session this record is about built `films/tram-nghi-nhieu-tap-03` ("Tại sao ta không tập trung được?") — a
20-minute episode of the channel, the first one that is not a short. It was watched while it worked, not after it
finished: a supervisor ran every fifteen minutes, put the film's own gate in front of it, and had a reviewer model
read the episode against the rules of the library.

This record is the second half of the retro: what the guardrails of the two earlier records did **not** catch, and
what now catches it. Three of the findings below were found **before** the episode was rendered, while they were
still cheap to fix.

## Context

- Film: `films/tram-nghi-nhieu-tap-03`, 101 → 175 spoken lines, target 20:00, Vietnamese (`vi-VN-NamMinhNeural`).
- The episode was started by copying the previous episode's folder (`cp -r tram-nghi-nhieu-tap-02 tram-nghi-nhieu-tap-03`).
- The pipeline: `core/render/review.sh` (filmcheck → drawcheck → claims → readcheck → whisper → stills → sheet → loudness),
  `tools/check_citations.py`, `tools/check_films_index.py` (new), `tools/check_claims.py` (new), `core/render/filmcheck.mjs` (new).
- The supervisor: `tools/supervise.sh`, every 15 minutes, its entries in `improvements/supervision/`.

## What went well

- The episode's **own** treatment, research file, title and credits were written before the first render — the copy
  was treated as a starting point and overwritten, not as the deliverable. That was the right instinct, and it is why
  the findings below are about *process*, not about the finished picture.
- The script grew with the film: 101 lines became 175 as the six chapters needed room. The timeline was generated
  (`mk_timeline.py`) rather than typed, so the voice and the timeline could not drift apart.
- The reviewer model (a 2.5-minute read of the whole episode) found the two number errors that no script in the
  library could see. It was right about them and wrong about nothing else — the one claim it could not verify (that
  `drawcheck` had reported a `BAD` line it never reported) it marked as unverified rather than asserting it.
- The 20-minute promise is now measurable: `films/README.md` carries the runtime, and `tools/check_films_index.py`
  compares it with the file that is actually there.

## Findings

### P1 — critical — A copied episode keeps the old episode's identity, and no check noticed

**Evidence.** The episode folder was made by copying the previous one — `cp -r tram-nghi-nhieu-tap-02
tram-nghi-nhieu-tap-03` at **08:05:54**, then `rm -rf out stills voice/proc …`. By 08:07:36 only `voice/lines.json`
had been replaced: `TREATMENT.md`, `CREDITS`, `index.html` and `research/FACTS.md` were still **byte-identical** to
tập 02's files — the treatment still argued about procrastination, the research file still headed "# Facts — Tập 02",
the page still carried tập 02's title. Nothing in the library looked.

The order is the finding. `AGENTS.md` says "**Treatment first, demo later**": write the treatment before you open
the previous episode's code. The session read `src/film.js`, `src/character.js` and `src/timeline.js` between
**08:05:12 and 08:05:32** and wrote its own `TREATMENT.md` at **08:14:35** — nine minutes after the shots it was
going to replace had been open in front of it. The session caught the copy by itself, but only because the author
remembered to rewrite the treatment; the episode would have shipped as a re-run of tập 02 with a new narration if it
had not.

**Guard now.** `core/render/filmcheck.mjs` (new): for every film folder it reports the identity files that are
byte-identical to another episode's (the older file is the source, the newer one is the copy that was never written),
and it reports every file that names an episode when they do not all name the same one. Wired into
`sh core/render/review.sh <folder>` (step 1), into `.github/workflows/films.yml` and into the pre-commit hook
(which runs it for every film with a staged file).

### P2 — critical — The build script kept the other episode's name, so the film would have shipped under it

**Evidence.** `films/tram-nghi-nhieu-tap-03/build.sh` still said `NAME=tram-nghi-nhieu-tap-02` for the first hour.
Every output of the build — the `.mp4`, the `.srt`, the poster, the transcript — would have been written under
tập 02's name, inside tập 03's folder, and the film index would have pointed at a file that does not exist.

**Guard now.** The first rule of `filmcheck.mjs`: the name the build writes the movie under must belong to no other
film of the library. The check reads the mux line of `build.sh` (with `$NAME` resolved) and compares it with every
other film's.

### P3 — high — A number the research file forbids was on screen

**Evidence.** The episode's `research/FACTS.md` says, in bold: **"9,5 phút không có trong nguồn nào"** — the number
9,5 minutes is in no source; it is 9 min 56 s for the dispersed group only, and the file allows the number on screen
**only** with the qualifier "nhóm phân tán". The shot list (`src/shots.js`) showed the card `num: '9,5', unit: 'phút'`
with the label "trước khi người ta tự nhảy sang việc khác" and no qualifier. The research file had already done the
work of forbidding it, in writing, and the film ignored it.

**Guard now.** `tools/check_claims.py` (new) reads the research file as sentences. A sentence that forbids a value
("không được nói", "không có trong nguồn", "Cảnh báo") supports nothing; a sentence that allows a value only with a
qualifier ("Nếu giữ … thì phải nói rõ …") demands that the qualifier be on screen too. Every card in the shot list
is checked against it. Wired into `review.sh`, into CI and into the pre-commit hook (for a film with a staged
research file).

**The check reads the markers it knows.** A research file that words its warnings differently is not covered by it —
the guard fails loudly on "Cảnh báo / không được nói / Nếu giữ … phải nói rõ" and stays quiet on other wordings.
That limit is stated here so the next session does not trust the check beyond its reach.

### P4 — high — A number was attributed to the year of a paper that does not contain it

**Evidence.** The card `num: '20', unit: 'phút', note: 'nghiên cứu năm 2009'` — the "before you switch tasks" figure.
The research file says the opposite in writing: the 20-minute figure belongs to **CHI '08** (Mark, Gudith & Klocke),
and the twenty minutes in Leroy (2009) **do not exist** — "con số 20,3 phút KHÔNG có trong bài Leroy (2009); nó là
số của một nghiên cứu khác (bài CHI 2008 của Mark)". The film's card moved a number from one paper to another.

**Guard now.** The year rule of `check_claims.py`: a card that attributes a finding to a year must find that year
beside that number in the research file.

### P5 — high — The wording of a number regressed, and only a reader could see it

**This is T10 again** — the finding the second record closed for tập 02 ("94 nghiên cứu" → "94 lần thử"), which
came back in the next episode. It is recorded there, as an update, with its evidence and its guard; this row is
only the pointer.

**Evidence.** The card says "94 **nghiên cứu**". The paper says "**94 independent tests**" — 94 trials, not 94
studies. The research file quotes the abstract: *"Findings from 94 independent tests…"*.

**Guard now.** `tools/check_claims.py` **reports** it (the research file words the number differently) but does not
fail: whether "94 nghiên cứu" is a lie or a fair shortening of "94 lần thử độc lập" is a reader's judgement. The
report names the pair; the judgement rule is now `CODING_STANDARDS.md` R6; the witness that sees it every fifteen
minutes is the supervisor. See [`retro/2026-10-30-tap-02.md`](2026-10-30-tap-02.md) T10.

### P6 — medium — The film index named a movie the build never wrote

**Evidence.** `films/README.md` lists `host-toon-test/phi-vu-triu-do-2-phut.mp4`. The file on disk — and the file the
build script writes — is `phi-vu-**trieu**-do-2-phut.mp4`. One letter. The index pointed at nothing.

**Guard now.** `tools/check_films_index.py` (new): every row of the index is checked against the file it names
(the runtime with `ffprobe`, the movie's existence), and every film folder must be in the index. Wired into CI.

### P7 — medium — A long job was polled with `sleep`, and the rule about it is only a rule

**Evidence.** The session waited for the voice with `sleep 45; … ls voice/*.wav | wc -l`. `CODING_STANDARDS.md` R5
says never to poll a long job with `sleep`; the harness delivers the job's output on its own. The rule was broken in
the first session too, and a rule the reader breaks is not a rule the reader has.

**Guard now.** `tools/supervise.sh` reads the running session's own tool log and reports every `sleep`, every
`git add -A` and every shell write it finds, next to the gate's output and the reviewer's findings. The rule now has
a witness that runs every fifteen minutes.

### P8 — medium — A class of defect that only an agent reading the film can see

**Evidence.** P3, P4 and P5 are all the same class: the picture says something the research file forbids, or words
it differently. No script in the library can decide that "9,5 phút" without "nhóm phân tán" is wrong, or that
"20 phút — nghiên cứu năm 2009" mixes two papers. The citation check sees only that a DOI exists.

**Guard now.** `tools/supervise.sh` runs a reviewer model over the episode every fifteen minutes, with the gate's
output, the session's own tool log and the film folder as its evidence, and asks for findings with their evidence
and the guard each one needs. Its findings are proposals: the supervisor writes them down, a human or the next
session verifies them. It found P3 and P4 while the episode was still being written.

### P9 — low — The channel's own production process was found by accident

**Evidence.** The session spent its first eleven tool calls rediscovering the library: `ls ~/lemo-my-styles/`, a
glob of the styles folder, a recursive grep for "nghĩ nhiều|overthink" across the whole repository, `ls -R` of two
film folders, `git log`, a search for the whisper model. The channel's own process — how an episode is made, where
the voice model lives, which folder each part of the pipeline lives in — is written down in
`films/tram-nghi-nhieu/CHANNEL.md`, and the session read it only by accident, as the eleventh file it listed.
It read the file at 08:03:45 and hunted for the whisper model anyway at 08:04:02–08:04:06: the information was
there, the pointer did not reach the reader.

**Guard now.** `AGENTS.md` gained a row for the case ("the user asks for another episode of a channel the library
already makes → the channel's `CHANNEL.md`"), and `TECHNIQUE.md` §13 names it as the map of the channel. No check can
decide which channel a request belongs to; the pointer is the whole fix.

### P12 — medium — The index promised a runtime the mix did not deliver

**Evidence.** `films/README.md` carries "20:00" for the episode, written before any render finished, from the
planned runtime the script printed. The mix that actually ran measured **1195.29 s** — 19:55. The index check
(`check_films_index.py`) fails when the file and the claim differ by more than 1.5 s, so the row would fail twice
for one reason: the movie was missing, and the runtime was wrong.

**Guard now.** `tools/check_films_index.py` compares the claimed runtime with `ffprobe` of the file and is in the
supervisor's gate, in CI and in the pre-commit hook. The row has to be written from the finished movie, not from the
plan.

### P13 — high — `pkill -f build.sh` killed the session's own renders

**Evidence.** Three times (08:49:27, 08:54:14, 08:59:12) the session ran `pkill -f "video.mjs" ; pkill -f "build.sh"`
from the repository root. The pattern matches **any** build in the repository, and the second one killed the
session's own render at about a quarter of the frames. `AGENTS.md` says: "Never kill processes you didn't start."
The session had the right instrument — `write proc://<id>/kill` — and used it successfully for a subagent.

**Guard now.** None — a pattern that kills by name cannot be caught by a check on the film. Recorded only, and the
reviewer model is the witness that saw it.

### P14 — medium — The drawing audit was skipped for one film and read as a pass

**Evidence.** `core/render/drawcheck.mjs` printed "**no rect helper** in films/host-toon-test; nothing to check" and
exited **0**. A film whose drawing code was never audited therefore passed the drawing gate — the same failure the
library already named ("a gate that cannot fail the build is decoration"), in the one place `readcheck` had already
solved it (it exits 2 with "NOT CHECKED — this is not a pass").

**Guard now.** `drawcheck` no longer stops at a missing helper: it says what it could not check ("no rect helper …
the rect rule does not apply; cel() and path() are checked") and goes on with the rules it can apply. The message is
the guard: an audit that says nothing is no longer a line that reads like a pass. The check still exits 0 for that
film — it has no `rect` rule to fail — and the limit is stated here so nobody reads the green as "audited".

### P15 — medium — The research file's own words are the authority, and the guard only knows some of them

**Evidence.** `tools/check_claims.py` reads the markers it knows: "Cảnh báo", "không được nói", "không có trong
nguồn" (a value is forbidden), "Nếu giữ … thì phải nói rõ …" (a value needs its qualifier) and the year a card
claims. A research file that words its warnings differently is not covered by the check, and the check's own name
("claims") promises more than it delivers.

**Guard now.** The limit is written down — here and in the check's header — so the next session does not trust it
beyond its reach. The two tiers are kept apart: a forbidden value or a misattributed year **fails**; a number the
file words differently is **reported**, because whether a shortening is honest is a reader's judgement.

## Open items

- The reviewer model's findings are **proposals**. The supervisor writes them down; it does not act on them, and one
  of its claims (that `drawcheck` had reported a bad line) could not be reproduced. Every finding a supervisor
  reports must be verified before it is fixed or recorded as a defect.
- `check_claims.py` reports a number the film words differently without failing. The judgement is a reader's, and
  the reporter that failed on this episode is the *human* one, three sessions later.
- The supervisor runs the reviewer model every fifteen minutes, which is a model call each time. It can be turned
  down (`SUPERVISE_LLM=on-fail` runs it only when the gate fails; `never` runs the gate alone).
- The 20-minute episode is the first one whose runtime is checked against the index. The other two films predate
  `check_films_index.py`; their runtimes are right today, but nothing was watching.
- **A name the film uses but never imports is a ReferenceError at render time.** The reviewer found it: the episode's
  `src/film.js` called `wrap()` — a helper `broll.js` exports — while its import line (copied from the previous
  episode) did not list it, and the page threw at the first card. The first attempt at a check was **reverted**: it
  reported 31 false positives on `films/host-toon-test` and 7 on `tram-nghi-nhieu-tap-02`, because the films import in
  forms the check did not understand (multi-line lists, `import * as S from './sets.js'` and the calls that go with it,
  names re-exported through a second module). A check is possible, but it must first read the import forms these
  films actually use, and it must be **quiet on all four films** before it may guard a build. Left undone on purpose.
- **The film's own shot checker says OK while three of the 175 lines have no shot.** `src/check_shots.py` names the
  uncovered lines (`c4_28, c5_24, c5_26`) and still exits OK — the "a gate that cannot fail the build is
  decoration" pattern, inside the folder that ships with the episode. The fix belongs to the episode's author: the
  checker should exit non-zero when a line has no shot, so leaving a line uncovered is recorded as a decision.
- **The card's note may contradict the line the card sits on.** The card "4 khối" carries the note "không phải mười
  bảy" while the voice says "Không phải bảy" — the picture and the voice give different numbers for the same fact.
  `check_claims.py` looks at the `num` of a card, not at the numbers inside its `label`, `note` or `sub`; a check
  would compare the card's own text with the line it is anchored to. Recorded only for now.
- **The research file's own references**: `tools/check_facts.py` (new) reports the duplicated bullets and the
  "mục 13" that points at the wrong section. It is wired into `review.sh` and CI, so the episode's research file
  now fails the gate until its author fixes it.
