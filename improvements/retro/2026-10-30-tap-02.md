# Retro — building Tập 02 with the new gate (2026-10-30)

The session that produced this record built `films/tram-nghi-nhieu-tap-02` ("Tại sao ta hay trì hoãn?") — the first film
made **after** the guards of [`2026-10-30-tram-nghi-nhieu.md`](2026-10-30-tram-nghi-nhieu.md) were put in place. The point of
this record is the other half of the retro: not what went wrong in the first session, but what the new guardrails caught,
and what they still do not catch.

## Context

- Film: `films/tram-nghi-nhieu-tap-02`, 1:42, 14 shots, 15 spoken lines, Vietnamese (`vi-VN-NamMinhNeural`).
- Pipeline: `core/render/review.sh` (the new one-command review), `core/render/drawcheck.mjs` (new),
  `tools/check_citations.py` (new), whisper check through `voice/asr_vi.py`.
- The film was written from scratch with the lessons of the first record applied on purpose: the thesis in the first ten
  seconds, the scope of every claim written down, a counter-case, and a CTA that is **acted out** on screen.

## What went well

- The read-time check caught a real defect before the film was finished (F1 below), and its report named the exact frame.
- `drawcheck` found nothing in the new code and reported it — a clean bill of health that used to require a human to
  look at every drawing call.
- The whisper check ran as part of the review and compared the script with the recording, line by line.

## Findings

### F1 — high — A label that changes every second is a new text piece every second, and the reading check says so

**Evidence.** The cold open counted down in the label itself: `'còn ' + mins + ' phút'`. The reading check keys every
piece of on-screen text by its text (`REG.id + ':' + s` in `src/broll.js`), so "còn 14 phút", "còn 13 phút", … were ten
different pieces, each on screen about 0.24 s, while the check requires roughly 2.2 s for a string of that length.

**Impact.** The review would have failed at the end of a full render — after the expensive part.

**Guard now.** The countdown is two fixed labels ("còn 15 phút" and "còn 5 phút") that switch at the word the voice says
them on (`w('hook', 12)`), so each label holds long enough; the shrinking bar carries the count itself, and a bar is not
text. **The general rule, now written down in this folder's `lessons.md`: an on-screen value that changes every frame
must not be rendered as text.** A value that ticks must be a shape, or a label that switches at a voice cue.

### F2 — medium — A marker highlight drawn after the words covers the words

**Evidence.** In the "nếu … thì …" card the `hilite()` strokes were drawn *after* the text, so the yellow and cyan bands
sat on top of the words instead of behind them (`films/tram-nghi-nhieu-tap-02/src/film.js`, shots `method` and `plan`).

**Guard now.** The order is fixed in the code: strokes first, words after. This one is still a human eye's job — the
reading check cannot see that a highlight covers its word — but the review contact sheet shows it immediately.

### F3 — medium — A set that fills the frame erases everything drawn before it

**Evidence.** `S.sofa()` starts with a full-frame fill, so drawing the character before the sofa erased him; the shot
came out empty. The fix was to draw the set first and the character on the seat.

**Guard now.** Fixed in the shot. No check knows which functions fill the frame.

### F4 — medium — Text inside a clipped panel is cut off, and the panel does not complain

**Evidence.** "TRÌ HOÃN KHÔNG PHẢI LỖI" at 78 px overflowed the card that clips it, so the film showed
"TRÌ HOÃN KHÔNG PHẢI L" — a title cut in the middle, with no error anywhere.

**Guard now.** Fixed in the shot (60 px). The lesson: measure the string against the box, not the box against the frame.

### F5 — high — A gate that cannot fail the build is decoration

**Evidence.** `build.sh` ended with `sh core/render/review.sh "$HERE" || echo "review.sh reported problems (see above)"`,
which turns a failed review into a successful build. The build now captures the status and exits with it.

**Guard now.** `films/tram-nghi-nhieu-tap-02/build.sh` (and the rule in `CODING_STANDARDS.md` R4: do not write "verified"
unless the output of the check is in hand).

### F6 — high — The review looked for the film in the wrong folder and skipped the loudness step

**Evidence.** `review.sh` looked for `*.mp4` in the film folder only; a fresh build writes to `<folder>/out/`, so the
loudness and black-frame steps printed "skipped" and the review still said PASS.

**Guard now.** `core/render/review.sh` now looks in both places and takes the newest file.

### F7 — medium — The whisper model sits on a drive that is often not mounted

**Evidence.** `~/.cache/huggingface` is a symlink to `/media/minhbien/EA92B68392B65433/omp-cache/huggingface`; when that
drive is not mounted, the download fails with a `PermissionError` that looks like a broken tool.

**Guard now.** The voice step of `core/render/review.sh` recognises the failure and prints
"skipped: no whisper model available" instead of failing silently — the check says what it did not do.

## Open items

- The reading check cannot see a highlight that covers its word, a word that means something else than it says, or a
  picture that contradicts the voice. The contact sheet and a human eye remain the last line.
- The review does not yet check that the subtitles match the recording word for word (only that the voice matches the
  script). A subtitle that drops a clause would pass.
